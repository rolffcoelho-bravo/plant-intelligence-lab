# Case Study B10-R — Support-Aware Forward-Time Environmental Geometry

## Purpose

B10-R diagnoses the failure exposed by B10 without changing the B9 forecast horizons and without selecting a replacement deployment model after seeing the test outcomes.

The question is:

\[
\boxed{\text{Can we identify why additional T2 environmental information becomes unsafe under forward-time deployment?}}
\]

B10-R separates two layers:

1. **Outcome-free support diagnostics**, computed using only each forward-year training environment set and the T0/T1/T2 environmental state available at issuance.
2. **Retrospective diagnostic analysis**, which relates those support measures to the already-observed B10 T2-versus-T1 error difference and probes a small, explicitly diagnostic rank/bandwidth grid.

No B10-R geometry configuration is promoted as a new champion. The sensitivity grid is an explanation tool, not a post-hoc deployment selection procedure.

## Support diagnostics

For each held-out environment B10-R measures:

- nearest training-environment distance in training-standardized environmental space;
- mean distance to the five nearest training environments;
- nearest-distance percentile relative to training-environment spacing;
- maximum RBF similarity to a training environment;
- local five-neighbor kernel density;
- total training-kernel mass;
- Nyström projection support and its residual;
- weather-only versions of the same support diagnostics;
- nearest historical geographic location;
- whether the city, exact rounded coordinate, and SSURGO map-unit identity were previously represented;
- training-kernel effective rank.

All scalers, distances, RBF bandwidths, eigenspaces and support quantities are derived from the corresponding training partition. Yield is not used to construct a support score.

## Year-level diagnosis

| Test year | Prior training environments | Mean environment T2−T1 RMSE | Fraction environments T2 worse | Median T2 max kernel similarity | Median T2 weather nearest distance |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2016 | **23** | **+2.914** | **72.2%** | **0.515** | **1.665** |
| 2017 | 41 | +0.667 | **77.8%** | 0.815 | 1.256 |
| 2018 | 59 | +0.000 | 42.9% | 0.813 | 1.406 |
| 2019 | 80 | -0.208 | 33.3% | 0.895 | 0.978 |
| 2020 | 101 | -0.144 | 37.5% | 0.943 | 1.076 |
| 2021 | 117 | +0.027 | 47.4% | 0.911 | 1.257 |

The 2016 environment set is the smallest historical support set and has substantially lower maximum training-kernel similarity than later years. It also has the largest T2 deterioration.

However, a relative nearest-distance percentile does **not** identify 2016 as unusually extreme: its median T2 percentile is about 0.35, compared with a median of roughly 0.44 across 2017–2021. This matters because a sparse training set can itself have wide internal spacing. Relative percentile alone can therefore hide absolute support weakness.

## Support-error association

The strongest pooled associations with environment-level `RMSE(T2) - RMSE(T1)` are:

| Support signal | Spearman correlation |
|---|---:|
| T2 maximum training-kernel similarity | **-0.392** |
| T2 weather nearest distance | **+0.356** |
| T2 weather mean-5 distance | **+0.345** |
| Number of prior training environments | **-0.342** |
| T2 weather maximum kernel similarity | **-0.341** |
| T2 weather local kernel density | **-0.330** |
| T2−T1 change in full nearest-distance percentile | **+0.309** |
| T2 full nearest distance | **+0.289** |

For the strongest pooled signals, leave-one-year-out estimates retain the same sign in every omitted-year analysis. This is useful evidence that the direction is not generated by one single year.

The within-year associations are much less uniform. The project therefore does **not** treat any one support variable as a validated abstention threshold. The result is better interpreted as evidence that **environmental support geometry contributes materially to transfer risk**, particularly across deployment years.

Within-year novelty quartiles are also non-monotonic in T2 deterioration. Nearest-environment distance alone is therefore insufficient for a deployment rule.

## Kernel geometry stress test

B10 used a frozen environmental representation with rank 16 and RBF gamma multiplier 2. B10-R evaluates a diagnostic-only grid:

- environmental ranks: 8, 16, 32;
- gamma multipliers: 0.5, 1, 2, 4;
- genomic rank remains 20;
- ridge alpha remains 10;
- forward-year splits remain unchanged.

This grid is **not** tuned or selected using the held-out outcomes.

### Pooled diagnostic sensitivity

| Diagnostic T2 geometry | Forward-year RMSE | Difference vs frozen T1 |
|---|---:|---:|
| rank 32, gamma 4 | **2.5392** | **-0.1222** |
| rank 8, gamma 1 | 2.6265 | -0.0349 |
| rank 8, gamma 0.5 | 2.6369 | -0.0245 |
| rank 32, gamma 2 | 2.6458 | -0.0156 |
| frozen B10 rank 16, gamma 2 | **3.2843** | **+0.6229** |

The lowest pooled diagnostic-grid RMSE is about **4.6% below** frozen T1. This does **not** become a new performance claim because the geometry was inspected using the same held-out outcomes. It establishes something different and important: the B10 T2 failure is highly sensitive to the environmental spectral geometry.

### The 2016 failure is largely geometry-dependent

The frozen 2016 T2 model has RMSE **6.6486**. In the diagnostic grid:

- rank 8, gamma 4: **2.2283**;
- rank 32, gamma 4: **2.2508**;
- rank 8, gamma 1: **2.3991**;
- frozen rank 16, gamma 2: **6.6486**.

The diagnostic minimum is about **66.5% lower** than the frozen T2 RMSE and about **18.3% lower** than the frozen 2016 T1 RMSE. Again, this is an oracle diagnostic, not a valid prospective model-selection result.

The frozen 2016 T2 training kernel has effective rank about **13.0** while the representation retains **16** dimensions. This is consistent with the hypothesis that the fixed rank/bandwidth choice exposes unstable spectral directions when only 23 historical environments are available. It is evidence of a rank-support interaction, not proof of the exact numerical mechanism.

### But geometry does not explain everything

2017 is decisive. Its training support grows to 41 environments and the T2 training-kernel effective rank rises to about 17.1, yet **none of the twelve diagnostic T2 geometries beats the frozen T1 RMSE** for that year.

Therefore the project should not reduce the B10 result to a simple statement that the original T2 model was badly tuned. The evidence supports a two-part diagnosis:

\[
\boxed{\text{T2 transfer risk} = \text{support/spectral geometry interaction} + \text{year-dependent information mismatch}.}
\]

The severe 2016 collapse is strongly geometry-sensitive. The 2017 T2 disadvantage persists across the tested geometry neighborhood. Later years are generally much less fragile and several T2 geometries outperform T1.

## Scientific conclusion

B10-R refines the B10 conclusion.

The data do **not** support either of these simplistic explanations:

- “T2 fails because there are too few environments.”
- “T2 fails because 60-DAP information is bad.”

Instead, the evidence indicates that the usefulness of later environmental information depends jointly on:

1. the amount and similarity of historical environmental support;
2. the spectral geometry used to represent that environment;
3. the deployment year and its weather-state distribution.

The strongest defensible statement is:

> Additional forecast-time-safe environmental information is conditionally valuable. Its deployment value depends on whether the historical support and environmental representation can map the new state stably; fixed spectral geometry can convert useful information into harmful extrapolation.

This is directly relevant to a biological decision system because it separates **information availability** from **information usability**.

## What B10-R does not establish

B10-R does not establish:

- a validated support threshold;
- a production abstention policy;
- that the diagnostic rank-32/gamma-4 configuration is a deployable champion;
- that the 2016 diagnostic oracle would have been chosen prospectively;
- a causal biological mechanism for the 2017 mismatch;
- that kernel effective rank alone predicts failure;
- that rank 16 is intrinsically bad;
- that T2 should always be used or always rejected;
- live prospective field performance.

The geometry sensitivity must now be converted into a **training-only selection problem** before any new T2 configuration or fallback policy can be admitted.
